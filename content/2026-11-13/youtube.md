## Script

**Scene 1: Hook**

This is a RM241 Bluetooth speaker shaped like a milk carton. And the internet can't agree on it. Half the reviews say it sounds way bigger than it looks. The other half say the bass is fake, it distorts when you turn it up, and the battery claim is a lie. So I tested all of it. By the end of this video you'll know if it's worth your money, and which setting you should never turn on.

**Scene 2: What it is**

This is the CMF Speaker S. CMF is Nothing's cheaper brand, so think Nothing design at a friendlier price. In Malaysia it starts from RM241. Inside there's a 45 mm driver, Bluetooth 6.0, and CMF says up to 18 hours of battery and IP68 water resistance. In the box you get the speaker and a quick-start guide. That's it. No cable. So if you're buying this as a gift, buy a USB-C cable with it, or it's a very pretty brick until someone finds a charger.

**Scene 3: Design and build**

Let's be honest, you're looking at this because of how it looks. And yes, it looks great on a desk. The shape makes you want to squeeze it, and one reviewer actually warns you not to. So I did, gently, for science. The fun part is that it's customisable. The knob and the panels come off, and CMF released the files so you can 3D print your own. Now the weak spot. Reviewers call this volume wheel the least convincing part of the speaker. Here it is up close. Listen to it turn, and you decide if it feels like RM241 or like a toy.

**Scene 4: Setup**

Pairing on Android is quick. Google Fast Pair pops up and you're done. On iPhone you don't get Fast Pair, so it's the normal Bluetooth menu. It works, it's just not as smooth. One more thing: there's no headphone-style 3.5 mm jack. Wired audio goes through USB-C instead. If you have an old laptop or a turntable with a 3.5 mm cable, keep that in mind.

**Scene 5: How it sounds at normal volume**

First, everyday listening. This is the volume most people actually use at home. Some reviewers say vocals sound a bit thin when it's quiet, so I picked a song that's mostly voice. Listen.

**Scene 6: Complaint one, the bass is fake**

Complaint number one: the bass is fake. There's a button for this called Ultra Bass. It's either on or off, no levels in between. Here it is at low volume with Ultra Bass off. And on. Now the same thing at high volume. Off. And on. One reviewer said Ultra Bass makes the rest of the sound worse. Listen to the vocals while I switch it, because that's where you'll hear it.

**Scene 7: Complaint two, it distorts when loud**

Complaint two: it falls apart when you turn it up. Reviewers say trouble starts around 60%. So let's walk it up. 50. 60. 70. 80. And all the way. Hear the moment the bass backs off? That's the speaker protecting itself. For a desk or a bedroom you'll never get near this. For a party outdoors, this is the number to know.

**Scene 8: Complaint three, the settings trap**

Complaint three, and this is the one nobody tells you about. In the app there's a setting called Wide Soundstage. Sounds great, right? Watch what happens when I turn it on. The EQ greys out. And Ultra Bass switches off. So if you spent ten minutes tuning your EQ and then turned this on, all that tuning is gone and the app doesn't warn you. My advice: pick one. Either Wide Soundstage, or your own EQ with Ultra Bass. Not both, because you can't.

**Scene 9: Complaint four, it's a toy**

Complaint four: it feels like a toy. Here's the full picture. It's light, it's plastic, and the wheel is what it is. But the panels pop off, you can swap the colours, and you can print your own. So it's less a toy and more a speaker that wants you to play with it. Whether that's a good thing depends on whether you're the kind of person who'd actually print a panel.

**Scene 10: Complaint five, 18 hours is a lie**

Complaint five: the 18-hour battery. Here's the small print. CMF got that number at half volume with Ultra Bass on. So I did a real day instead. Full charge this morning. Music on and off all day, at the volume I actually like. And this is what's left tonight. A full recharge takes about two and a half hours, so it's an overnight thing, not a quick top-up before you leave the house.

**Scene 11: Water and Party Mode**

Two quick ones. Water: CMF says IP68, which means fresh water up to 1.5 metres for 30 minutes. That's their claim. I couldn't find anyone who's tested it properly yet. Should I? Tell me in the comments. Second, Party Mode. It uses Auracast, so you can play the same music on several speakers at once, and reportedly even on some speakers from other brands. Reviewers who paired two of these said they sound full and wraparound. Which, I'll admit, is a smart way to sell you two.

**Scene 12: Is it worth RM241?**

So, is it worth RM241? Here's how I'd decide. If you want something that looks great on your desk, sounds bigger than its size at normal volume, and you'll mostly listen indoors, this makes a lot of sense. Just skip Wide Soundstage if you care about your EQ. If you want loud music outdoors, a big bass-heavy sound, or a 3.5 mm jack, spend a bit more on something else. As for the five complaints: some are true, some are overblown, and one is a setting you can avoid in two seconds.
